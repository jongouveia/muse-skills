  ## Purpose
  Give a new business idea a hard look before it costs money or months. The skill runs the idea through five stress tests, names the weakest assumption behind it, and returns a plain verdict: go, fix first, or drop it. It also ranks the fixes that would change the verdict and lays out a one-week plan of cheap real-world tests, so the next step is evidence, not more thinking.

  ## Workflow
  1. Take the idea in plain words: what it sells, who pays, and why now.
  2. Run five stress tests, one at a time: customer, money, competition, founder fit, and timing. For each, ask the hard question the idea has to survive.
  3. Name the single weakest assumption behind the idea, and say what would prove or break it.
  4. Give a verdict: go, fix first, or drop it, with one line of reasoning.
  5. Rank three fixes by impact, starting with the one that changes the verdict fastest.
  6. Build a one-week validation plan: a cheap test for each open question, with what to measure and what result would change your mind.

  ## Output Contract
  - The verdict: go, fix first, or drop it, with one line of reasoning.
  - Five stress test results: customer, money, competition, founder fit, and timing, each with a pass, weak, or fail mark and one sentence why.
  - The weakest assumption, named plainly, with the test that would prove or break it.
  - Three fixes ranked by impact.
  - A one-week validation plan: the test, what to measure, and the result that changes the verdict.

  ## Operating Rules
  - Never soften a bad verdict. A weak idea gets a weak verdict, with the reasons.
  - Never make the decision for the user. The verdict is advice; the user decides.
  - Ask for missing facts once, then test with what you have. Do not stall on details that do not change the verdict.
  - Keep every test cheap and real: a conversation, a landing page, a pre-order, a quote. No test should cost real money or a week of build time.
  - Do not give legal, tax, or regulated financial advice.

## What it includes

Every business idea feels solid until someone asks the hard questions. This skill asks them for you, before the idea costs money or months. It runs the idea through five stress tests: customer, money, competition, founder fit, and timing. For each one it asks the question the idea has to survive, then marks it pass, weak, or fail with one sentence of reasoning. It names the single weakest assumption behind the idea and the test that would prove or break it. Then it gives a plain verdict: go, fix first, or drop it, with one line of reasoning, and ranks three fixes by impact, starting with the one that changes the verdict fastest. Finally it builds a one-week validation plan of cheap real-world tests, a conversation, a landing page, a pre-order, or a quote, with what to measure and what result would change your mind. The verdict is advice, not a decision; you decide what to do with it.

## Example

You want to sell a subscription box of vinyl record cleaning supplies to collectors. The skill tests it: customers exist and pay for record care, the money works at $25 a month only if churn stays low, competition is real from two established shops, founder fit is strong because you already collect, and timing is fine. The weakest assumption is that collectors will pay monthly for supplies they buy twice a year. The verdict is fix first. The top fix is to sell a one-time starter kit with an optional refill plan instead of a forced subscription. The one-week plan starts with asking ten collectors what they spend on cleaning supplies each year.
